WEATHER RESEARCH IDEAS & READING NOTES
=====================================

Public working notes — Joseph Lockwood
Canonical filename: weather_research_ideas.txt

Purpose
-------
A working research log on atmospheric predictability, ensemble forecasting,
weather regimes, stratosphere–troposphere coupling, North American temperature
risk, and energy-relevant forecast confidence.

The central research question is not only:

    What does the forecast say?

but also:

    Under the current atmospheric state, how much should the forecast be trusted?

A useful operational framework is therefore:

    atmospheric state
        + regime / trajectory
        + ensemble agreement
        + forecast history
        + physical precursor support
        -> P(forecast is trustworthy)


1. FLOW-DEPENDENT FORECAST TRUST
--------------------------------

Ensemble spread alone is not a complete uncertainty measure. Forecast skill can
vary substantially with the large-scale circulation state. The aim is to build
conditional verification such as:

    E[|forecast error| | atmospheric state, lead]

rather than relying only on unconditional RMSE or ensemble spread.

Potential inputs:
    - weather regime and regime-transition probability
    - North Pacific jet state and trajectory
    - Rossby-wave packet structure
    - MJO / ENSO / QBO
    - stratospheric polar-vortex state
    - WN1 / WN2 amplitude and phase
    - ensemble agreement / entropy
    - run-to-run consistency


2. NORTH PACIFIC JET TRAJECTORIES
---------------------------------

Winters (2021) motivates using a two-dimensional North Pacific jet phase space:

    PC1: jet extension <-> jet retraction
    PC2: poleward shift <-> equatorward shift

The key operational idea is to verify the TRAJECTORY through this space, not
only the final state. Large errors can arise from:

    - wrong transition
    - wrong amplitude
    - wrong persistence
    - wrong decay timing

Retraction / equatorward-shift configurations are particularly interesting
because they are linked to North Pacific ridge amplification and relatively
large forecast errors while also being relevant to North American cold risk.

Suggested features:
    NPJ_PC1_mean(t), NPJ_PC2_mean(t)
    NPJ_PC1_spread(t), NPJ_PC2_spread(t)
    P(extension), P(retraction)
    P(poleward_shift), P(equatorward_shift)
    dPC1/dt, dPC2/dt
    transition timing spread
    trajectory distance between successive runs


3. WEATHER-REGIME AGREEMENT AS A CONFIDENCE FILTER
---------------------------------------------------

Bloomfield et al. (2021) show that large-scale circulation patterns can be used
to identify windows of opportunity for more skillful energy forecasts.

Their simple conditioning rule keeps detailed grid-point forecasts when at
least half the ensemble agrees on the same large-scale weather regime.

Important caveat:

    high agreement != guaranteed correctness

Agreement should therefore be calibrated by regime, lead time, transition type,
and historical reliability. A stronger version uses regime entropy:

    H = -sum_j p_j log(p_j)

and estimates:

    P(forecast verifies | agreement, regime, lead, transition)

rather than applying a universal confidence threshold.


4. STRATOSPHERIC WAVE-1 AS A WINDOW OF OPPORTUNITY
--------------------------------------------------

Ding et al. (2026) identify a predictable wave-1-like lower-stratospheric mode
at 100 hPa that is associated with enhanced North American surface prediction.

Key reported results:
    - week-2 North American SAT skill improvement ~15%
    - week-2 RMSE improvement ~14%
    - cold-extreme risk over North America increases ~50% in ERA5
    - regional cold-extreme frequency skill improves ~28% relative to controls

The physical sequence is approximately:

    tropospheric ridge precursor
        -> strong lower-stratospheric WN1 growth
        -> regional negative 100-hPa eddy heat flux over eastern North America
        -> asymmetric downward wave coupling / reflection-like behavior
        -> Pacific Trough -> Alaskan Ridge transition
        -> eastern North American trough / cold
        -> enhanced conditional forecast skill

Operational variables to track:
    A1_100, phi1_100
    A1_50,  phi1_50
    A1_10,  phi1_10
    vertical phase tilt
    regional 100-hPa eddy heat flux
    regional Plumb / Takaya-Nakamura Fz
    ensemble spread in WN1 phase and amplitude
    run-to-run changes in phase and coupling timing

A scalar WN1 amplitude is not enough:

    amplitude = HOW STRONG
    phase     = WHERE
    vertical tilt / Fz = WHICH WAY PROPAGATING


5. TWO DOWNWARD-COUPLING PATHWAYS
---------------------------------

A useful conceptual split from Perlwitz & Harnik and subsequent literature:

A. Zonal-mean / absorptive pathway

    upward planetary-wave forcing
        -> stratospheric absorption
        -> vortex weakening
        -> lower-stratospheric anomaly
        -> NAM / polar-cap descent
        -> tropospheric circulation response

Track:
    U10,60N
    U100,60N
    polar-cap Z at 10 / 50 / 100 / 500 hPa
    100-hPa heat flux
    persistence / descent

B. Asymmetric / reflection-like pathway

    WN1 upward forcing
        -> regional reflection / downward propagation
        -> tropospheric wave restructuring
        -> regional ridge / trough response

Track:
    WN1 phase
    vortex displacement longitude
    regional Fz near 70–100 hPa
    regional heat flux
    Pacific Trough / Alaskan Ridge transition

The practical distinction is:

    Type 1 asks: how far DOWN does the zonal-mean anomaly penetrate?
    Type 2 asks: WHERE does the downward wave activity occur?


6. ROSSBY WAVE BREAKING AND REGIME TRANSITIONS
----------------------------------------------

Michel & Riviere (2011) show that Rossby wave breaking is not merely a
fingerprint of a weather regime; it can participate in regime formation and
transition through eddy momentum forcing and jet shifts.

Important distinction:
    AWB -> poleward momentum flux / tendency toward a more poleward jet
    CWB -> equatorward momentum flux / tendency toward a more equatorward jet

A useful forecast diagnostic is therefore not only whether strong RWB exists,
but whether the EXPECTED RWB pattern supporting the current regime is breaking
down — potentially serving as an early transition-warning feature.


7. RUN-TO-RUN FORECAST EVOLUTION
--------------------------------

Successive forecast cycles contain information. Important diagnostics include:

    - divergence between consecutive ensemble distributions
    - ensemble reconvergence
    - regime-probability revision
    - trajectory revision
    - transition-timing revision
    - persistence of minority ensemble clusters

The goal is to distinguish normal ensemble noise from a dynamical sign that the
model is struggling with an unresolved transition.


8. IMPACT-AWARE CIRCULATION TYPES
---------------------------------

Traditional regimes maximize separation in circulation space. Targeted
circulation types instead classify states according to an impact variable or
impact vector.

Possible North American energy vector:

    X = [PJM load,
         ERCOT load,
         MISO load,
         gas HDD,
         wind,
         solar,
         net load,
         price-stress proxy]

Useful comparison:
    meteorological weather regimes
    vs impact-targeted circulation types
    vs hybrid patterns balancing forecastability and impact separation

A pattern with a strong impact relationship is not automatically useful if the
forecast system cannot predict the pattern reliably.
