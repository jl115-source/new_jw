IDEAS

What I am tracking.

01

Forecast trust, not just forecast value

Build flow-dependent confidence models that estimate when an ensemble forecast deserves more or less trust, using atmospheric state, ensemble structure and forecast history.

02

North Pacific jet trajectories

Track extension, retraction and meridional shifts of the North Pacific jet in phase space. The transition path and persistence of a regime may be more informative than the endpoint alone.

03

Regime consensus as a confidence filter

Use large-scale regime agreement to condition detailed surface forecasts. High agreement is not automatically correct, so confidence should be calibrated by regime, lead time and transition type.

04

Stratospheric wave-1 opportunities

Track lower-stratospheric wave-1 amplitude, phase, vertical tilt and regional wave flux as a possible window of opportunity for North American cold and improved week-2 forecast skill.

05

Asymmetric downward coupling

Separate zonal-mean vortex descent from regional wave coupling. For North America, longitude, wave phase and where downward wave activity occurs can matter as much as vortex strength.

06

Run-to-run forecast evolution

Measure successive-run divergence, trajectory shifts and ensemble reconvergence. Large revisions may contain information about unresolved regime transitions and forecast error.

07

Impact-aware circulation types

Compare meteorological weather regimes with impact-targeted circulation types. A useful pattern must balance physical predictability with its ability to separate meaningful surface or energy outcomes.

08

Rossby-wave structure & extremes

Track wave-packet amplitude, propagation and phase errors as possible precursors to large forecast revisions, regime transitions and underprediction of temperature extremes.

FRAMEWORK

A WORKING VIEW OF FORECAST CONFIDENCE

Atmospheric state → forecast difficulty → surface impact.

The recurring idea across these papers is that ensemble spread alone is not enough. A useful forecast-confidence system should combine large-scale circulation state, regime transition, wave structure, stratospheric coupling, run-to-run stability and calibrated ensemble agreement.

STATE + TRAJECTORY + ENSEMBLE AGREEMENT + FORECAST HISTORY → P(FORECAST IS TRUSTWORTHY)
